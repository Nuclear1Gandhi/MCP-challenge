Differentiation strategies for this lesson
Before reading — vocabulary preview
Vocabulary at Grade 3; pre-teaching key words lowers the cognitive load so Jasmine can focus on meaning, not decoding.

Before whole-class reading begins, pull Jasmine (or a small group) for a 3-minute vocabulary walk. Show the word, say it together, give a student-friendly definition, and have her draw or write one quick example.

IEP: repeat directions IEP: reference sheets IEP: small group
During reading — chunked annotation with a sentence stem
Jasmine often does not annotate independently; breaking text into short chunks with a single focus per chunk builds the habit without overwhelming stamina.

Give Jasmine the 4-step annotation guide (in the student copy below). After each paragraph chunk, check in briefly: "What did you write? Does it match the question?" This is the IEP-mandated 1:1 check-in built into instruction.

IEP: reminders to pause, plan, proceed IEP: 1:1 check-ins ELA goal: annotate accurately for prompt
During reading questions — graphic organizer scaffold
Jasmine is beginning to see the value of graphic organizers; pairing the during-reading questions with an organizer turns open-ended writing into a fill-in process she can manage.

Use the "T-chart" scaffold below for Question C (Turn & Talk / list 3 traits of community). Jasmine records one trait per row in her own words — this also primes her for the short-answer at the end.

IEP: graphic organizers and checklists IEP: copy of teacher notes ELA goal: answer literal questions about text
Independent practice — choice + self-checklist
Peer motivation and class jobs are Jasmine's strengths; offering structured choice reduces avoidance and increases ownership. The self-checklist mirrors her IEP goal of independently using supports.

Allow Jasmine to respond to the short-answer question using one of two formats: (A) written paragraph with graphic organizer, or (B) oral response recorded by teacher/aide with Jasmine later copying her own words. Either path requires claim + one piece of evidence + one sentence of analysis — the current short-term benchmark.

IEP: extra time IEP: scheduled breaks IEP: multimodal student output ELA goal: write a claim that answers the question
Student-led discussion — peer leverage
Jasmine is warm, peer-motivated, and loves to help others — discussion is a natural strength and a low-stakes confidence builder.

Strategically pair Jasmine with a patient, verbal partner. Assign her Question 1 (courteous behavior in a community you belong to) — the most personal and concrete prompt — so she can draw on lived experience rather than text recall alone.

IEP: small group (as needed) IEP: reminder to remain engaged
Regulation & engagement check-ins (embedded in lesson)

Scheduled breaks

After During-Reading Questions and before Independent Practice (~20 min mark). Offer a movement break (stand, stretch, switch seats) — aligns with IEP counseling goal of using calming strategies.

Specific positive praise

Name exactly what Jasmine did: "I noticed you re-read paragraph 5 before answering — that's exactly what strong readers do." Avoid general praise. Frequency: at least twice per lesson.

Watch for shutdown signals

Head down, repeated restroom requests, silence after engagement. Prompt quietly: "Pause, plan, proceed — what's one thing you can try right now?" Do not wait for full disengagement.

Small group option

Per IEP (Jasmine chooses small group 80% of the time when offered). Have the SE teacher or inclusion aide positioned nearby during independent practice to facilitate a small group pull if needed.
